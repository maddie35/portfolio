## Ten years explaining things. Eight making them learnable.

As a technical writing veteran, I have worked on a range of projects and documentation. My ultimate passion lies at the intersection of education/learning and accessible documentation. 

I write documentation for people who are already frustrated. That's the honest description of the job because nobody reads documentation for fun. They arrive because something didn't work or because they have to do a thing they've never done, and every extra word between them and the answer is a small tax on someone's bad afternoon.

For the last decade, I've written developer and API documentation, release notes, reference material, and the process documentation that keeps all of it true after launch. Mostly for technical products in regulated industries: enterprise software, consumer lending, engineering organizations where the subject matter experts know the material cold and have never once thought about how a stranger encounters it.

The learning design came later and changed how I write. If you've had to prove someone can actually do something afterward, you stop being satisfied with documentation that is merely accurate.

## What I believe about this work

### Documentation is a product, not an artifact
Something you ship once and never revisit isn't documentation, it's a snapshot. The interesting problems are the operational ones: what triggers an update, who owns accuracy, how you find out something went stale before a customer does. I built an AI-powered monitor that watches code deploys and flags product changes with documentation impact because waiting for someone to file a ticket is not a system.

### The hard part is what you leave out
Completeness is the easy instinct and usually the wrong one. Every sentence that isn't load-bearing pushes the answer further down the page. Getting to the version that a stressed user can actually use requires analysis, conversations with stakeholders, and cutting material you were proud of.

### If you can't run it, don't document it
For API and developer docs, I make the calls myself. Not because I don't trust the SME, but because the gap between how something is described and how it behaves is exactly where users get stranded, and you only find that gap by walking through it.

### Accessible writing is just better writing
Clear structure helps both a screen reader user and the person skimming at 4:55pm. Plain language helps a non-native speaker and the novice engineer reading on their phone. WCAG 2.1 AA isn't a remediation pass at the end; it's the constraint that makes the first draft better.
